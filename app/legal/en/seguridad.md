# Diving safety

Last updated: {{version}}

Diving involves **risks**. This page summarises basic precautions. It does not replace training or the instructions of the centre and the guide.

## Before booking

- Dive only within the **limits of your qualification** and recent experience. If you have not dived for a while, ask for a refresher dive.
- Make sure you are in **good health**. If in doubt (heart or respiratory conditions, pregnancy, medication, recent surgery…), consult a diving medicine specialist before diving. The centre may ask you to fill in a medical questionnaire.
- Have **diving insurance** covering the activity and evacuation.

## Dive site information is for guidance only

Depths, levels, coordinates and access are based on public and centre information but **may not be exact**, and conditions change (currents, visibility, waves, boat traffic). Marine reserves may require permits and have specific rules. The final decision on whether and where to dive is made by the centre and the guide on the day.

## On the day

- Arrive in good time, rested and without having drunk alcohol.
- Check your equipment and your buddy's before entering the water.
- Always follow the **briefing** and the guide's or instructor's instructions.
- Respect no-decompression limits and safety stops.
- Do not fly or travel to high altitude in the 24 hours after diving.

## Protect the sea

Do not touch or take anything, control your buoyancy to avoid damaging the seabed and respect the rules of protected areas.

## In an emergency

In Spain and the EU, call **112**. For diving accidents, the centre will activate its emergency plan and direct you to the nearest hyperbaric chamber.
